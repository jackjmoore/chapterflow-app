As it is today. Starting is a modal of three number tiles and two checkboxes. While it runs, the only sign is a small pill at the far left of the footer, showing time left and the word “stop”; there is no sense of how far through you are or how much you have written. The dimming option fades the menus, search, rail and binder but leaves the toolbar and footer bright, so the window reads as half-asleep rather than focused. The result comes back as another modal.

Clearer means it tells you, in words, what is happening. Starting opens a dialog that first says what a sprint is, then offers the lengths as plain buttons. While it runs, one sentence sits between the toolbar and the page, at the page's own width, and keeps itself current: time left, words so far, and the way out. A hairline under it fills from left to right. Nothing else in the window changes, and there is no dimming option: focus is the other mode's job. When time is up, the same line reads out the result and offers another; there is no modal to dismiss.

Write without stopping for a while

A sprint is a timer. When it runs out, you'll see how many words you added while it ran, and the sprint is kept with the writing session it happened in.

Clearer means there is less to read. A sprint is treated as time-boxed focus: choosing a length in a small popover by the footer is all there is to it, and for as long as it runs the chrome goes away entirely, as in distraction-free mode. The window's bottom edge becomes the timer, a thin line filling from left to right, with one centred line of figures above it. Hovering there reveals the way to stop. When time is up the chrome comes back on its own and the footer says what happened, in one sentence, until you next type. No dialog opens at either end.
