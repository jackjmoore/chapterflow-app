Custom colors

Replaced by the cards above: a theme you make here is the same four wells, kept under a name, drawn on the wall like any other.

A theme has a room of its own. Below the wall, "Your themes" is a plain list; making or editing one opens a workbench in the same section: controls down the left, the proof on the right. The proof is a full window with real manuscript text, the binder, a selection, a footnote marker and the footer, painted from the four colours, so every surface the theme will touch is on screen at once. Light and dark are two tabs on the same theme, each with its own four colours, and one sentence beneath the wells says how the text reads against the background and the ink against the page. The rest of the app is not repainted until you save, which keeps the workbench calm. Empty state: one line and a button.

How it will look

Real text from the open document, with a selection, a footnote and a running sprint, so every surface the theme touches is here.

The preview is the control. Making a theme puts one large window on the page, wearing the candidate, and you click the thing you want to change: the binder for the background, a heading for the text, the open document's name for the accent, the sheet for the page. A colour popover opens where you clicked, and the whole window follows as you drag. Four small callouts name the parts the first time, and go once each has been touched. The name is the heading above the window, typed straight in. Saved themes are chips under the window; choosing one wears it, and the same window is where you edit it. There is no form.
