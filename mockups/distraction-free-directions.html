Distraction-free mode · fake data

As it is today. Every piece of chrome disappears: menus, search, rail, binder, toolbar and footer. What is left is the page, the scrollbar, and a small “Exit focus” pill pinned under the window's caption buttons. The word count, page count and save status go with the footer, so there is no way to tell how the session is going without leaving. The mode is called “Distraction-Free Mode” in the View menu and “focus” on the pill, and its shortcut, Ctrl+Alt+F, is also the shortcut for Insert › Footnote, which wins. The three states on the right make no difference here: the mode looks the same throughout.

Clearer means the mode explains itself once, then keeps quiet. The pill goes; Escape leaves, and the mode is called “focus” everywhere, including the View menu. On entering, one line appears in the window's bottom margin, set in the interface type: how to leave, and how the session stands. It fades after a few seconds and comes back whenever the mouse moves or you pause. The page is otherwise exactly the page: same width, same sheets, same surround, with a faint vignette at the window's edges so the paper reads as the lit thing. Nothing about the text changes.

Clearer means the page itself shows where your attention is. Everything from A, and then two things on the paper. The paragraph you are writing in stays at full ink; the rest of the page is set back to a softer grey, and the line with the caret is held at a fixed height in the window as you type, so the text moves and your eyes do not. At the foot of the sheet, where a printed page would carry a folio, the document's count and page sit in the manuscript's own type: “546 words” and “page 2 of 2”. The only control is a small “Focus” mark in the corner, which on hover says how to leave.
